## Per-talk mechanics inventory

### 1. Sam Herd — "A Poor Start—A Rich Ending" (May 2018)

> "I was born in a one-room log cabin in a very small town called Liberty, Indiana, U.S.A."
> 🏷  **Bourdain — climactic-moment opener** (concrete particular as
> first beat — log cabin / Liberty / Indiana — not topic sentence).
> Also **Tippett — formative-origin opener** at the macro level — the
> talk *is* origin-shaped.

> "A sister in our congregation, whose husband was an unbeliever,
> knocked on our door early one morning and handed me an envelope. It
> was full of money. She and several of her young children had been
> getting up very early in the morning to go out in the fields and
> chop cotton — removing the weeds growing between the rows — in order
> to earn enough money so that I could take the trip to New York."
> 🏷  **Single fully-rendered scene** (Herd technique #2). One scene,
> sensory specifics (early morning / envelope / cotton field).
> 🏷  **Bourdain — specificity past the comfort zone** (the
> parenthetical "*removing the weeds growing between the rows*" — one
> notch beyond "chopped cotton"; the scene is unforgettable because
> of the unnecessary precision).

> "Gloria was a jewel then, and she is a jewel today."
> 🏷  **Sam Herd — parallel-clause inversion** (then/today).
> Time-collapse landing — Mr. Rogers' "reaches across time" mechanic.

> "Over the years, I have seen that prejudice is not really about a
> person's skin; it is about sin — the Adamic sin that infects us all."
> 🏷  **Clear — two-noun pivot** (skin → sin, with surgical phonetic
> compression). Same word-shape, opposite direction. Rotation lives
> in a single phoneme swap.

> "My life may have had a poor start materially, but the ending is
> spiritually rich, far richer than I could ever have imagined!"
> 🏷  **Naval — pursuit-order reframe** wrapped in **Sam Herd
> parallel-clause inversion**. The reorder isn't of events but of
> measurement: poor materially → rich spiritually.

### 2. Anthony Morris III — "Remembering My First Love for Jehovah" (May 2015)

> "IT WAS early summer in 1970. I lay in my hospital bed at the
> Valley Forge General Hospital, Phoenixville, Pennsylvania, in the
> United States."
> 🏷  **Bourdain — climactic-moment opener** but with **declarative-
> military register** (full place-name string, hospital bed). Same
> mechanic as Herd, different texture.

> "the smell of burned human flesh and the sight of blood and gore"
> overwhelmed him as he recalled young soldiers asking, *"Am I going
> to make it?"*
> 🏷  **Hart — pain-as-character.** The wartime trauma is rendered as
> sensory image, not abstract grief. Borrowable as: when the weight
> is heavy, render the sensory specifics, not the emotion.

> "What I really appreciate about her is that while she loves me, she
> loves Jehovah more."
> 🏷  **Sam Herd — parallel-clause inversion** (loves me / loves
> Jehovah more). Different speaker, same mechanic — confirms the
> mechanic is generic across GB.

> "Jehovah is so kind! He takes care of his faithful worshippers."
> 🏷  **Theatrical-exclamation landing.** Anti-Tyler. Documented in
> calibration.md as a Morris-only move ("the avoid list"). Confirms
> the contrast: Morris closes on exclamation; Herd closes on quiet
> certainty.

## Talk-level patterns (what makes a JW talk remembered)

### Hypothesis 1: ONE compressed image carries the whole talk — **CONFIRMED**

The clearest pattern. Lett's parakeet IS his "forever" talk. Splane's
highway IS his 2021 talk. Sanderson's "love people / make disciples"
brochure imperative IS his 2023 talk. Winder's "principles are like
light" IS his 2025 talk. Talks remembered for years across the
community are remembered because *one image survives* — the image
becomes a token the community can pass around without needing the
rest of the talk.

This generalizes to a corollary: **talks without a single carry-
image rarely survive in community memory.** Doctrinal talks (Splane
2023's "Trust in the Merciful Judge") are remembered for the
*announcement*, not for sustained quotation. Image-talks are
remembered for sustained quotation.

### Hypothesis 2: Aphoristic phrase that lives in audience memory — **CONFIRMED**

Verified at the speaker level (Herd, Sanderson, Sam Herd's "Gloria
was a jewel"; Winder's "we are not embarrassed about adjustments";
Sanderson's "Love People — Make Disciples"). The aphorism is what
travels — even when no one remembers the talk's logical structure,
the phrase is repeated. *The aphorism is the talk's archive
mechanism.*

### Hypothesis 3: Perspective-inverting reframe — **CONFIRMED**

Splane's "Trust in the Merciful Judge" reframes "Sodom and Gomorrah
are NOT resurrected" → "we don't know." Winder's "How does the light
get brighter?" reframes "The GB has been wrong sometimes" → "Light
gets brighter gradually — that's how it always was." Sanderson's
"Love People, Make Disciples" reframes "memorized presentation" →
"talk like Jesus did." Reframe is consistently the *rotation slot*
mechanic at AM-scale.

### Hypothesis 4: Permission given that other speakers don't give — **CONFIRMED with refinement**

The talks that survive give permission the audience didn't expect.
Winder 2023: permission to admit the GB is fallible. Splane 2023:
permission to *not know*. Herd 2018 (life story): permission to have
felt regret over a promise. Jackson 2024: permission to be surprised
by changes (named in title). Winder 2025: permission to think in
principles rather than rules.

**Refinement: the permission is always pre-emptive.** The speaker
names the audience's unspoken doubt or reluctance and *resolves it
first*, before delivering content. This is the **Voss labeling
mechanic at talk-scale.**

### Hypothesis 5: Specific structural shape (problem → personal story → biblical figure → application → aphorism) — **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, with two competing shapes**

Two structural shapes show up consistently:

**Shape A — Herd / homiletic shape:**
small-window opener → fully-rendered domestic scene → quiet
scripture-phrase lock → parallel-clause aphoristic landing.

**Shape B — Splane / structural-exegesis shape:**
biblical-text image as opener → verse-by-verse exposition with
contemporary applications laid over → doctrinal pivot or
announcement → quieter close.

Both shapes are remembered. Shape A is more *quotable*. Shape B is
more *doctrinally consequential*. The 5-step shape proposed in the
prompt is closer to Shape A; Splane talks tend to skip the personal-
story slot.

### New hypothesis 6: AM talks marry image to announcement

When an AM talk lands an image to a doctrinal change, it sticks.
Sanderson's "Love People, Make Disciples" is the brochure launch —
the brochure itself becomes the talk's image-token. Lett's parakeet
sticks because it carries doctrinal weight (the magnitude of
"forever"). The image without the announcement is just illustration;
the announcement without the image is just news. Both together =
durable talk.

## Annual Meeting–specific patterns (2018–2025)

### Pattern 1: "Adjustments" framing as doctrinal-change carrier

> "We are not embarrassed about adjustments that have been made if
> we don't get it exactly right." — Winder, AM 2023

> "Don't Be Surprised by Sudden Changes" — Jackson, AM 2024 (talk
> title)

The word *adjustments* is the AM-2018-onward replacement for "new
light." It carries less doctrinal claim and more humility. The GB
has been **migrating its self-naming vocabulary** — old: "the truth";
mid: "new light"; recent: "adjustments / clarifications." The
direction is toward less-finality. Tyler should track this — the
voice the GB itself models is moving toward Tyler's existing
register (Voss-labeling, permission-leading) and away from Morris-
era exclamation.

### Pattern 2: Direct second-person interrogative titles

A surprisingly stable AM-talk-title shape:
- "Are You Completely Sober?" (Jackson, AM 2019)
- "What Do We Have to Fear?" (Sanderson, AM 2019)
- "Will You Care for It?" (Cook, AM 2019)
- "Are You a Joyful Person?" (Herd, AM 2023)
- "How Does the Light Get Brighter?" (Winder, AM 2023)
- "Don't Be Surprised by Sudden Changes" (Jackson, AM 2024)
- "Everlasting Life — Possible? Boring?" (Lett, AM 2024)

This is **NEPQ tonality at title scale.** The title is a question
the audience must answer inside their own head before the talk
begins. The talk's content is the speaker's answer overlaid on the
audience's already-formed answer. By the time the audience gets to
the rotation, they're already two beats deep.

Compare to declarative-statement titles common at conventions:
"Always Rejoice!", "Pure Worship", "Declare the Good News!"
Convention themes are imperatives; AM talks are questions. The
register difference is real.

### Pattern 3: Permission-by-uncertainty as the rotation

Multiple recent AM talks land their rotation on **"we don't know"**
or equivalent.
- "We don't have the answers to those questions" (Splane, AM 2023)
- "We can't just be dogmatic" (Splane, AM 2023)
- "We can't be dogmatic. It's above our pay grade." (Jackson, AM 2023)
- "The Governing Body is neither inspired nor infallible" (Winder, AM 2023)
- "We just don't know, Jehovah will do the right thing" (recurring AM 2023)

This is a **proposed new mechanic**: *permission-by-uncertainty*.
The rotation isn't a counter-claim; it's the *abandonment* of a
claim. The audience receives permission to release a previously-
held position. Compare to Morrison's *refusal as method* —
similar structural move, different doctrinal context. Voss's
labeling-the-feeling generalizes here: name what the audience is
already uncertain about, then say *we are too*.

### Pattern 4: Compressed-image hooks

Confirmed strongly. AM talks since 2021 have leaned on a single
image: the highway, the chariot, the flashlight, the fingerprint of
love, the parakeet. The image is announced early and recurs as
load-bearing reference through the talk. Talks without an image
(e.g. straight doctrinal-symposium pieces) read as duty-talks; talks
with an image read as gift-talks.

### Pattern 5: We-they-us pronouns

Less prominent than expected. The dominant pronoun migration in AM
talks is **GB → "we" (the GB speaks) → "you" (the audience) → "we"
(all of us together).** Tyler's existing pronoun migration (I → we)
matches the *audience-comment* version of this — borrowed from
Baldwin's I-you-we. AM speakers do something close, but the *we* is
institutional, not personal. Tyler should NOT borrow the
institutional-we — it sounds platform-formal.

### Pattern 6: First-line sound-bite

Several AM talks open with a short declarative claim:
- "This is truly a historic day for Jehovah's witnesses." (Sanderson 2023)
- "Now 10 years later exactly, we have arrived at another true landmark." (Cook 2023)

This is the **Hormozi sound-bite claim** at talk-opening scale. It
flags stakes before content. Audience knows: this one matters.
Compare to convention talks, which often open with scripture-
address (Fleegle-style). AM talks open with claim. The contrast is
real.

## Mechanics that scale down from 30-min talk to 30-sec comment

Mapped to the four-slot architecture from `drafting-recipe.md`.

### Slot 1 (Opener) — scales DOWN

| AM/talk mechanic | Comment-scale equivalent |
|---|---|
| Compressed-image hook (parakeet, fingerprint, highway) | One-image opener — *"What strikes me is..."* + the image |
| Direct interrogative title ("Are You a Joyful Person?") | NEPQ-style opener — *"Have you ever noticed..."* |
| Sound-bite claim opener (Sanderson 2023) | Hormozi sound-bite opener already in toolbox |
| Climactic-moment opener (Herd 2018, Morris 2015) | Already in toolbox; works at any length |

The image hook scales down most cleanly. *One image, one comment* is
already Tyler's discipline (fridge, sidewalk flower, grandma's ring).
Confirmation: the AM-talk image-discipline is the same discipline,
in 30x footprint.

### Slot 2 (Label/Parity) — scales DOWN STRONGLY

| AM/talk mechanic | Comment-scale equivalent |
|---|---|
| Adjustments framing ("we are not embarrassed about adjustments") | Voss labeling — *"It can feel like..."* |
| Permission-by-uncertainty ("we don't know") | Bourdain self-implication — *"I'd be lying if I said..."* |
| Pre-emptive title-permission ("Don't Be Surprised by Sudden Changes") | Mr. Rogers possession-without-condition — *"It's hard. We don't have to pretend it isn't."* |

This is the *strongest* scale-down. The AM permission-by-uncertainty
move is exactly the slot-2 Voss-labeling move at talk scale. Tyler
already does this at comment scale; the mechanic is identical, just
40x footprint.

### Slot 3 (Rotation) — scales DOWN, with caveat

| AM/talk mechanic | Comment-scale equivalent |
|---|---|
| Reframe-by-renaming (Sanderson "memorized presentation" → "talked with people") | Clear two-noun pivot |
| Permission-by-uncertainty rotation (Splane 2023) | Miner reframed-question landing |
| Two-noun pivot (Winder "laws / principles") | Already in toolbox |
| Frame refusal (Sanderson refusing tract-script frame) | Morrison frame refusal |

Caveat: AM talks often deliver TWO rotations (a reframe + an
announcement). Comment-scale forbids two rotations (per drafting-
recipe Rule). Pick ONE.

### Slot 4 (Landing) — scales DOWN BEST

| AM/talk mechanic | Comment-scale equivalent |
|---|---|
| Sam Herd parallel-clause inversion ("jewel then / jewel today") | Already the default landing |
| Aphoristic compression (Rumph "leave a fingerprint of love") | Holiday Marcus-style aphorism |
| Two-question title resolution (Lett "possible? boring?") | NEPQ landing — answer the question you opened with |
| Three-word philosophy ("Keep It Simple") | Dungy three-word philosophy as identity |

The 30-min-to-30-sec scaling is cleanest at the landing slot. The
aphoristic landing IS the one-line takeaway; the talk and the
comment are *the same shape* at the closing. This is why aphoristic
landings travel — they were sized for memory all along.

### Net mapping

The four-slot architecture **already encodes the scale-down**.
Tyler's comments and the AM-talk shape both run:

```
[image-hook]  →  [permission-label]  →  [reframe-rotation]  →  [aphoristic-landing]
```

The AM talk fills each slot with 5–8 minutes of content; Tyler
fills each slot with one sentence. Same architecture, different
footprint. The mechanic toolbox is unchanged; what changes is the
density per slot. **This is the load-bearing finding: the AM-talk
patterns confirm the four-slot architecture rather than challenging
it.** Tyler can borrow shape directly from AM talks because the
talks operate inside the same architecture, just bigger.

## Proposed additions to the 12-mechanic toolbox

After this pass, three mechanics surfaced that aren't fully covered
by the existing 12. Naming them.

### Proposed mechanic 13 — **Compressed-image-as-spine**

Used by: Lett (parakeet), Splane (highway), Sanderson (fingerprint),
Anthony Morris (diabetes/family-as-house — calibration.md Sec. 6),
Sam Herd (cotton field).

Distinct from existing image-discipline because it works at *talk
scale*, not just *paragraph scale*. The image isn't decoration — it
is the *spine*. Pull the image, the talk has no skeleton.

**Borrow rule for Tyler:** when commenting on a paragraph, ask: *is
there ONE image carrying this whole comment, or am I leaning on
two?* If two, cut one. The discipline is identical to the AM-talk
discipline at 30x scale.

**When to deploy:** every comment. This is closer to a principle
than a slot-level mechanic. Already half-encoded in Principle 1
(concrete carries abstract); this names it more sharply.

### Proposed mechanic 14 — **Permission-by-uncertainty**

Used by: Splane (AM 2023), Winder (AM 2023), Jackson (AM 2023, 2024).

The rotation isn't a counter-claim; it's the abandonment of a claim.
Audience receives permission to release a previously-held position.
Distinct from Voss labeling (which names the *feeling*) — this names
the *limit of what is known*. Closer to Morrison's frame-refusal,
but with humility-tone instead of refusal-tone.

**Borrow rule for Tyler:** when the article speaks confidently about
something audiences quietly worry isn't actually clear, the rotation
can be *we don't know — and that's the answer*. Tyler hasn't used
this yet. It's a high-trust move — only deployable when the comment
has the standing to name uncertainty without sounding shaky.

**When to deploy:** Slot 3 (Rotation) — but rare, and only after
audience parity is fully earned in Slots 1–2. Probably one comment
per quarter, not per week.

### Proposed mechanic 15 — **Two-question / interrogative-title pre-empt**

Used by: Lett ("Possible? Boring?"), Jackson ("Are You Completely
Sober?"), Sanderson ("What Do We Have to Fear?"), Winder ("How Does
the Light Get Brighter?").

The *form of the title or first sentence* is two stacked questions
that name the audience's likely objections. The talk's content
defuses both. Compresses two NEPQ moves into one container.

**Borrow rule for Tyler:** when the paragraph addresses two related
audience hesitations, the opener can stack them as questions —
*"Is it really possible? And if it is — does it last?"* — and the
rotation answers both. Use sparingly — stacked questions can feel
rhetorical-platform if the comment isn't disciplined.

**When to deploy:** Slot 1 (Opener) — alternative to single-question
opener. Maybe twice a quarter.
