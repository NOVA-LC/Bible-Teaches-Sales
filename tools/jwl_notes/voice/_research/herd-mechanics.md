### Source 1 — *Watchtower*, May 2018 (already in calibration; deeper tagging here)

Re-tagged at the mechanic level — earlier calibration noted these
passages but did not slot them against the 12-mechanic toolbox.

> "I was born in a one-room log cabin in a very small town called
> Liberty, Indiana, U.S.A." **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Bourdain — climactic-moment opener** *(adapted)*. Herd opens at
> the smallest spatial unit ("one-room log cabin") and the smallest
> place ("very small town called Liberty, Indiana"). The opener is a
> visual zoom rather than a thesis statement. This is the same
> "start-where-the-heat-is" move Bourdain uses to open at the
> river-crossing rather than the political backstory.

> "He never made much money, but he gave us a place to live, clothes
> to wear, and enough food to eat." **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Brown — definitional triple-naming** *(domesticated)*. Where
> Brené Brown triple-names abstractions (*"uncertainty, risk, and
> emotional exposure"*), Herd triple-names household economics
> (*"a place to live, clothes to wear, and enough food to eat"*). The
> mechanic — three short noun phrases, no adjectives, third item
> closes the rhythm — is identical; only the register is domestic.

> "Gloria was a jewel then, and she is a jewel today. To me, she is
> more precious than the Hope Diamond." **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Sam Herd parallel-clause inversion** (the toolkit's named
> default landing; instance #1 of the most distinctive Herd move).
> Note the *then/today* axis — a temporal inversion rather than a
> conceptual one. This is its own sub-shape; see the proposed
> mechanic in the additions section.

> "My life may have had a poor start materially, but the ending is
> far richer than I could ever have imagined." **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Clear — feels-like inversion** *(life-arc form)* PLUS **Sam
> Herd parallel-clause inversion**. Clear's *"Motion feels like
> progress. Action is progress."* uses the same surface-vs-truth
> inversion Herd applies to material-vs-richness. The Herd-specific
> overlay is the autobiographical scope: the inversion is over an
> entire *life*, not over a moment.

> "You may wonder why Jehovah's Witnesses would separate black and
> white publishers. And the answer is that we had no choice back in
> those days." **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Tippett — formative-origin question** + **direct rhetorical
> question with embedded answer**. Tippett asks origin questions to
> lower the speaker's defensiveness; Herd asks an origin question of
> his own audience ("you may wonder *why*…") and answers it himself,
> giving permission for the audience to have asked the same thing
> silently. This is a permission move masquerading as a transition.

> "I learned that a true follower of Jesus 'needs to be gentle toward
> all.'—2 Timothy 2:24" **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Scripture-paraphrase lock** (toolkit signature; instance from
> earlier calibration). Mechanic-level: Bourdain's preposition-pivot
> compresses inversion onto a single small word; Herd's
> scripture-lock compresses authority onto a single phrase pulled
> from the verse. Same compression discipline, different target.

> "I have learned that a brother is a brother regardless of his skin
> color, and he will die for you if necessary." **(verbatim, jw.org)**
> 🏷 **Sam Herd parallel-clause inversion** *(definitional form)*.
> "A brother is a brother" is a self-defining tautology that lands as
> revelation because the second clause widens the definition by
> action ("die for you"). Mechanic: the definitional tautology +
> consequential clause. See proposed mechanic.

### Source 2 — JW Broadcasting July 2017, "Love—The Identifying Mark"

Spoken Herd at length. The Tumblr transcript yields these patterns:

> "Welcome to the program, brothers and sisters! During the next hour,
> you will hear the story of a brother who previously protected the
> president of a country." **(verbatim via Tumblr)**
> 🏷 **Bourdain — climactic-moment opener**. He opens not on the
> theme (Christian love) but on the most dramatic specific scene the
> hour will deliver (a brother who guarded a head of state). Same
> rule as the spaghetti opener of Tyler's ¶14: open one notch
> sideways from the topic.

> "It is more than being a nominal Christian — a Christian in name
> only." **(verbatim via Tumblr)**
> 🏷 **Clear — two-noun pivot** *(redefinition form)*. Two near-terms
> (a real Christian / a "nominal" Christian) pivoted apart by an
> em-dash gloss. Compare Clear's *"This isn't preparation. This is
> panic dressed up as preparation."*

> "Not specifically dying for each other, but living for each other."
> **(verbatim via Tumblr — confirms an earlier calibration quote)**
> 🏷 **Sam Herd parallel-clause inversion** (toolkit default).
> Mechanic-level: this is also a **Bourdain preposition-pivot** in
> miniature — the inversion sits on the swap between *dying* and
> *living* (one-syllable verbs, gerund form), with the rest of the
> sentence preserved. The clause-mirror plus the verb-pivot together
> are why this line is so frequently quoted by Herd's audience.

> "What kind of love would that be?" **(verbatim via Tumblr)**
> 🏷 **Perel — name-the-unnamed-dimension** *(question form)*. Names
> the dimension the listener feels but won't articulate (*the
> insufficiency of the love we're tempted to settle for*). The rhetorical
> question makes the audience name it inside their own head.

> "But we do not have to be an elder, do we?" **(verbatim via Tumblr)**
> 🏷 **Mr. Rogers — possession-without-condition** *(in
> inverse-question form)*. Tells the listener: the love-mark belongs
> to all of you, not only to those with title. The "do we?" tag is a
> permission grant — the same gesture as Mr. Rogers' "I like you just
> the way you are."

> "When was the last time you acted hospitably?" **(verbatim via
> Tumblr)**
> 🏷 **Tippett — formative-origin question** *(direct-pointed
> form)*. The question is small enough to be answered silently in
> the listener's own memory. Formative-origin shape: not "do you act
> hospitably" (defensive), but "when was the last time" (a date and
> a memory).

> "Yes, love truly is the identifying mark of true Christians. That's
> what stands out when you visit other congregations, perhaps when
> traveling." **(verbatim via Tumblr — closing lines)**
> 🏷 **Hormozi — compression-expansion** *(reverse form)*. Where
> Hormozi opens with a compressed claim and unfolds, Herd lands with
> a compressed claim ("love truly is the identifying mark") and then
> issues *one* concrete proof image ("that's what stands out when
> you visit other congregations"). The ratio is reversed but the
> structure is the same.

## Distinctive Herd mechanics (signature)

Five moves emerge from the corpus that are particularly Herd's — not
universal patterns, not shared with Splane, Lett, Cook, or Sanderson
in this density.

### H1 — The temporal-axis inversion

Across the corpus, Herd's parallel-clause inversions disproportionately
pivot on **time** rather than concept:

- *"Gloria was a jewel then, and she is a jewel today."* (then / today)
- *"Twenty years ago we didn't know the generation. But now we know
  all about the generation."* (twenty years ago / now)
- *"Let us be content to stay in Jehovah's house — not just for a
  short while but forever."* (short while / forever)
- *"My life may have had a poor start materially, but the ending is
  far richer than I could ever have imagined."* (start / ending)

Other GB members who use parallel-clause landings (e.g., Lett,
Splane) more often pivot on *concept* (right vs. wrong, true vs.
false, organized vs. disorganized). Herd's most distinctive landings
pivot on *time*. The implication: the move feels like life-witness
rather than argument. **Add to toolkit as a Herd-specific landing
sub-form.**

### H2 — The household-economy verb-list

Herd's three-beat lists (the "Brown triple-name" mechanic in the
12-toolkit) are **disproportionately built from household-care
verbs or domestic resources**, not abstractions:

- *"a place to live, clothes to wear, and enough food to eat"*
- *"keeps us warm, protects us, and gives us refreshing waters of
  truth"*
- *"Christians are to be kind and considerate, mild-tempered and
  patient, and are to exercise self-control"* (the three pairs are
  themselves household-virtue clusters)

This is a *register* signature — same mechanic as Brown's triple-
naming, but Herd consistently chooses **physical-care verbs over
abstract nouns**. Tyler's "fridge / spaghetti / brother" instinct is
operating in the same register tradition.

### H3 — The permission-tag interrogative

Herd ends statements with a small interrogative tag that grants the
listener permission to push back, to feel the difficulty, or to share
the sentiment without being instructed:

- *"But we do not have to be an elder, do we?"* (do we?)
- *"That's a lot, isn't it?"* (isn't it?)
- *"Don't you just love their zeal and joy?"* (don't you just?)
- *"Were you surprised that we read that verse?"* (open audience-
  reaction question)

This is **not** Tippett's "however you think of that" — it's a
shorter, oral, contraction-heavy English tag. It functions the same
way (lowering defensiveness, granting permission) but it's
register-specific. **Propose as a new mechanic** (see additions
below).

### H4 — Direct rhetorical question + immediate self-answer

Herd consistently asks himself the question the listener was
preparing to ask, then answers it before the listener can voice it:

- *"You may wonder why Jehovah's Witnesses would separate black and
  white publishers. And the answer is that we had no choice back in
  those days."*
- *"What is it?"* (followed by the verse he reads)
- *"How does Jehovah enable us to flourish?"* (followed by *"In the
  spiritual paradise, God keeps us warm…"*)

The mechanic differs from Miner-style reframed questions (which let
the listener arrive at the answer themselves). Herd asks **and**
answers — but the answer carries because the question was *the
listener's question first*. He's modeling permission to wonder.

### H5 — The "I learned that…" scripture-application sentence

Herd's preferred move for embedding a scripture is not "as Paul
said…" or "the Bible teaches…" — it's a first-person learning
declaration:

- *"I learned that a true follower of Jesus 'needs to be gentle
  toward all.'"*
- *"I have learned that a brother is a brother regardless of his
  skin color, and he will die for you if necessary."*

The mechanic: **lead with personal admission of learning, lock the
verse phrase as the lesson, then implicitly invite the listener to
share the learning.** This is closest to Bourdain's
**self-implication first** mechanic (dismantle authority before
asserting it) but applied to scripture rather than critique. The
audience trusts a teacher who says "I learned" before "you should."

## Proposed additions to the 12-mechanic toolbox

### Proposal A — "Herd permission-tag interrogative" (sub-mechanic of Mr. Rogers / Tippett)

> **Definition.** A short, oral, contraction-heavy interrogative tag
> appended to a declarative statement, which grants the listener
> permission to share the sentiment, push back, or notice their own
> reaction — without being instructed.
>
> **Form.** *[Declarative claim], [contraction-tag]?*
> — *"But we do not have to be an elder, do we?"*
> — *"That's a lot, isn't it?"*
> — *"Don't you just love their zeal and joy?"*
>
> **When to reach for it.** Slot 2 (Label/Parity) when the audience
> needs permission to nod silently before the next move; or Slot 4
> (Landing) when a softer, oral-register close is wanted.
>
> **Distinguished from.** Tippett's permission tag (*"however you
> think of that"*) is longer, written-register, and follows a
> question. Herd's tag is shorter, oral-register, and follows a
> declarative.

### Proposal B — "Herd temporal-axis inversion" (sub-mechanic of Sam Herd parallel-clause)

> **Definition.** Parallel-clause inversion landing whose pivot is
> *time* rather than concept — *then/now*, *short-while/forever*,
> *start/ending*, *twenty-years-ago/today*.
>
> **Form.** *[Subject] was [X] [past-marker], and [Subject] is [X]
> [present-marker].* / *Not just [Y] for [short-time], but [Y] for
> [long-time].*
>
> **When to reach for it.** Slot 4 (Landing) when the comment is
> testifying to *continuity* — that something has been or will be
> true across a span. The temporal axis carries weight that
> conceptual inversions can't.
>
> **Distinguished from.** Conceptual parallel-clause landings
> (*"What stands in the way becomes the way"*) operate on the *same
> moment*; Herd's temporal landings operate across *spans*.

### Proposal C — "Domesticated triple-naming" (Herd-register variant of Brown)

> **Definition.** Triple-naming compression where the three terms are
> physical-care verbs or household resources rather than abstract
> nouns.
>
> **Form.** *[verb of domestic care], [verb of domestic care], and
> [verb of domestic care].* / *[domestic resource], [domestic
> resource], and [domestic resource].*
>
> **When to reach for it.** Slot 3 (Rotation) when the abstraction
> needs to be re-grounded; or Slot 4 (Landing) when the triple itself
> is the rule.
>
> **Distinguished from.** Brown's triples (*"uncertainty, risk, and
> emotional exposure"*) abstract upward. Herd's triples concrete
> downward. Tyler's natural register is already this variant.

### Proposal D — "Personal-learning scripture lock" (extension of scripture-paraphrase lock)

> **Definition.** Embed a scripture phrase via a first-person
> learning declaration ("I learned that…", "I have learned that…")
> rather than via direct citation ("the Bible says…").
>
> **Form.** *I learned that [paraphrase / locked phrase from verse].
> — [citation]*
>
> **When to reach for it.** Whenever scripture is being applied,
> especially when the article is mid-doctrinal-argument and the
> commenter wants to add experiential weight.
>
> **Distinguished from.** Scripture-paraphrase lock alone leaves the
> attribution implicit; Herd's "I learned" form *adds* the speaker's
> own admission of having had to learn it. Bourdain self-implication
> applied to scripture.

## Implications for Tyler's drafting

Five actionable instructions, mapped to slot.

### T1 — Slot 1 (Opener): zoom in spatially before zooming in conceptually

Herd opens *"in a one-room log cabin in a very small town called
Liberty, Indiana"* — three increasingly small spatial frames before
the topic appears. Tyler's instinct ("when I go to the house with my
parents") already does this. **Sharpen the move:** when opening a
comment, name the *smallest physical container* of the scene first
(the kitchen, not the house; the fridge, not the kitchen; the
spaghetti, not the fridge). The room leans in because the camera is
already close.

### T2 — Slot 2 (Label/Parity): use the Herd permission-tag interrogative

When the comment carries audience-shared weight that needs
acknowledging, append a short oral-register tag. **"…right?" /
"…isn't it?" / "…you know what I mean?"** These tags are the
spoken-register equivalent of Voss labeling. Use one per comment,
no more. Note Herd's deliberate use of the contraction (*don't
you just*) — the contraction is part of the disarmament.

### T3 — Slot 3 (Rotation): ask the listener's question, then answer it

Herd's *"You may wonder why … And the answer is that…"* is a
permission move dressed as a transition. Tyler's natural rotation
("So maybe the question isn't 'do I see the value' — it's…") is
*Miner-style reframed question* (let listener arrive themselves).
**Add to the rotation toolbox:** Herd's variant — *ask their
question, then answer it directly*. Use this when the audience
question is small and concrete; reserve Miner's reframed question
for rotations the audience needs to complete in their own head.

### T4 — Slot 4 (Landing): when in doubt, pivot the landing on time, not concept

When drafting a parallel-clause landing, ask first: **does this
inversion run across a span?** If yes, write the temporal version
(*"X was Y then, and X is Y today"* / *"not for [short-time] but for
[long-time]"*). If no, write the conceptual version. Across a year
of comments, Tyler's temporal-axis landings will accumulate as a
distinct cadence — one of Herd's signature contributions to the
voice tradition.

### T5 — Slot 4 (Landing) alternative: lead with "I learned" when the verse is doing the work

For comments whose rotation hinges on a scripture-application,
default to the **personal-learning scripture lock** form. *"I
learned that…"* + locked phrase + citation. This is Herd's most
trusted move because the audience cannot resist the testimony of
someone admitting they had to learn it. Tyler's *"that was Jesus"*
