### Stephen Lett

**Sources accessed**

- Stephen Lett, parakeet/Grand Canyon "forever" illustration. Special
  Assembly Day, Iowa (date unconfirmed in this pass; widely circulated
  excerpt). Quoted text below from secondary text-archive of the
  spoken excerpt; treat the *shape* as primary, the exact line-breaks
  as approximate.
- Stephen Lett, 2021 Regional Convention Review (faith / apostate-driven
  lies talk). Verbatim quotes preserved at
  https://beroeans.net/2021/10/05/lett-2021-convention-review (text
  repository for the spoken video). Lett spoke at length; the lines
  below are pulled from that transcript.
- Stephen Lett, Wikipedia summary cross-reference.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lett

**Mechanics inventory (3–5 tagged passages)**

> "A tiny parakeet… gets one tiny grain of sand in its beak… flies
> over the Grand Canyon and drops it in… Now you can imagine how long
> it would take to fill up a teaspoon. But when that process is
> repeated over and over again, until the Grand Canyon is totally
> filled… When that happens… forever has just barely begun."
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Cumulative-escalation hypothetical.** A single
> hypothetical scaled in stages until the listener's ability to picture
> it breaks; the breaking is the point. Different from Bourdain's
> sensory specificity (which renders ONE moment in detail) — this is
> ONE absurd unit (a parakeet, a grain) iterated until the scale
> overflows comprehension. Closest in the existing toolbox to Brown's
> triple-naming or Hormozi's compression-expansion, but the engine
> is *exhaustion of imagination* rather than rhythm.

> "If our faith makes us powerful, we will fully believe all of
> Jehovah's promises, no matter how extraordinary they may seem.
> We'll do so without doubting whatsoever."
> 🏷 **Hormozi sound-bite claim** (Slot 1) — single compressed
> declarative used as the talk's anchor; "no matter how extraordinary"
> + "without doubting whatsoever" is the absolute-modifier signature
> Lett leans on (cf. Hormozi-style certainty without hedges).

> "Well that is foolish thinking indeed. That would be like reasoning
> if I eat stinky, rotten food from a garbage can it would really
> help me in the future to recognize bad food."
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Reductio-by-vivid-image.** Lett doesn't argue
> against the position; he restates it as a sensory absurdity ("stinky,
> rotten food from a garbage can"). The image *is* the refutation.
> This is one notch sharper than Morrison frame-refusal — Morrison
> refuses the frame in language; Lett refuses it by replacing the
> abstraction with an image so undignified the listener can't keep
> holding the original idea.

> "Think about the apostate-driven lies and dishonesties that
> Jehovah's organization is permissive toward pedophiles."
> 🏷 **Polarization mechanic** — Lett uses absolute-stance polemic
> (cf. the Andrew Tate technique noted in calibration.md, but trained
> on ideas rather than groups). Names the opposing claim, refuses to
> entertain it, advances. Intensity is the move.

**Signature move (confirming brief's characterization)**

The brief says: *"Lett: vivid hypothetical with cumulative escalation
(parakeet)."* Confirmed. But this pass identifies a second signature:
**reductio-by-vivid-image** (the garbage-can move). Both share an
underlying engine — Lett's preferred rhetoric is *image as
non-rebuttable evidence*. He is not arguing; he is replacing thought
with picture. That's why the parakeet works on a Witness audience
even though the math doesn't actually work.

The confirmed signature for Tyler's purposes: **cumulative-escalation
hypothetical** stays as Lett's distinctive mechanic. Treat the
reductio-by-vivid-image as a related technique in the same family
(image-as-argument).

### David Splane

**Sources accessed**

- David Splane. "Jehovah Has Trained Me From My Youth." *The
  Watchtower (Study Edition)*, April 2026. jw.org.
  https://www.jw.org/en/library/magazines/watchtower-study-april-2026/David-Splane-Jehovah-Has-Trained-Me-From-My-Youth/
- David Splane. "Types and Antitypes" talk, 2014 Annual Meeting,
  October 4, 2014. Verbatim quotes from the official Annual Meeting
  Report at jw.org plus secondary transcript repositories
  (ad1914.com).

**Mechanics inventory (3–5 tagged passages)**

> "I STARED at the slip of paper that the brother had just handed
> me. On it were the words, 'David Splane, April 8, 1953:
> "Proclaiming the World's Doom."' 'What's this?' I asked. The
> brother answered, 'This is your assignment to present a talk in the
> Theocratic Ministry School.' I protested, 'I didn't sign up for
> this!' But I am getting ahead of myself."
> 🏷 **Bourdain climactic-moment opener** — Splane opens at the
> hot moment (the slip of paper, the protest), then explicitly stops
> and rewinds: *"But I am getting ahead of myself."* He's using the
> opener mechanic AND naming his use of it. That naming-of-the-move
> is itself a Splane signature: he frequently surfaces structure
> rather than hide it.

> "He had taken a course in public speaking and was fascinated by
> what he heard at the meeting. My dad decided to attend that meeting
> every week. Little by little, he began to attend other meetings."
> 🏷 **Sequential-progression compression** — Splane compresses a
> timeline into three short sentences with explicit progression
> ("decided to attend… week. Little by little…"). The cadence is
> *step-by-step* rather than image-by-image. Closest existing mechanic:
> Hormozi compression-expansion, but Splane's beats are temporal
> (then → then → then) rather than parallel claims.

> "Where the Bible makes a clear connection between a type and an
> antitype, we gladly accept it. But where the Bible is silent, we
> must be silent."
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Permission-and-prohibition pair.** A balanced
> rule stated as two parallel clauses bound by a single principle.
> Different from Bourdain preposition-pivot (one sentence inverts on
> a small word) — this is two sentences, each a complete rule, both
> derived from the same upstream commitment. Splane's structural
> trademark.

> "Who is to decide if a person or an event is a type if the word of
> God doesn't say anything about it?"
> 🏷 **Miner reframed-question** — but with Splane-specific texture:
> the question carries the answer inside its grammar. *"If the word
> of God doesn't say anything about it"* makes refusal the only
> possible reply. Tyler's reframed-question move asks the listener to
> arrive at the answer themselves; Splane's leaves no room for
> arrival anywhere else.

> "If we get caught up in looking for typical and antitypical patterns
> and fulfillments, we may lose sight of the practical, everyday
> lessons that Bible accounts can teach all of us."
> 🏷 **Clear two-noun pivot** in slow motion — *patterns* vs.
> *practical, everyday lessons*. Splane builds the binary across a
> full sentence rather than compressing it ("Motion vs. Action").
> This is the same mechanic at half speed.

**Signature move (confirming brief's characterization)**

The brief says: *"Splane: structural-analytical exegesis with verb-tense
precision."* Confirmed and refined. Splane's deepest signature is
**permission-and-prohibition pair** (X-yes-but-Y-no, both rules from
the same upstream commitment). The 2014 talk's load-bearing line —
*"Where the Bible makes a clear connection between a type and an
antitype, we gladly accept it. But where the Bible is silent, we must
be silent."* — is the cleanest example of his architecture. He
operates inside a frame of *what the text licenses vs. what it forbids*.

This is the move Tyler doesn't have natively. Tyler reframes; Splane
*adjudicates*. Both deliver a rotation, but Splane's rotation lands
because the audience feels they've been given a clear rule.

### Geoffrey Jackson

**Sources accessed**

- Geoffrey Jackson. "Let the Many Islands Rejoice." *The Watchtower*,
  August 15, 2015. jw.org.
  https://www.jw.org/en/library/magazines/w20150815/let-many-islands-rejoice/
- Geoffrey Jackson, statement to Australian Royal Commission into
  Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse, August 14, 2015.
  https://www.childabuseroyalcommission.gov.au/sites/default/files/STAT.0670.001.0001.pdf
  (Used as cross-check on his spoken voice in non-pulpit register;
  not pulled in full this pass.)

**Mechanics inventory (3–5 tagged passages)**

> "It was a day I will long remember. I was with a number of brothers
> from different parts of the world, and we nervously waited in the
> Governing Body conference room. The Writing Committee was about to
> enter, and we had been assigned to make a presentation. During the
> preceding few weeks, we had analyzed problems that translators were
> facing, and now we had to recommend solutions. It was May 22,
> 2000. But why was this meeting so important? Before I explain, let
> me tell you something about my background."
> 🏷 **Bourdain climactic-moment opener** + **Tippett formative-origin
> question** — Jackson opens at the heat (a specific room, a specific
> date, a specific assignment), then uses an explicit hinge ("Before
> I explain, let me tell you something about my background") to pivot
> to origin. This is a *two-mechanic chain* in the opener slot;
> Jackson uses it consistently.

> "Learning Tuvaluan was not easy. The only book available in the
> language was the 'New Testament.' There were no dictionaries or
> language courses, so we decided to try to learn from 10 to 20 new
> words each day. But we soon realized that we did not understand
> the correct meaning of most of the words we were learning. Instead
> of telling people that divination was wrong, we were in fact telling
> them to avoid using measuring scales and walking sticks!"
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Concrete cultural-detail anecdote with mistake
> on-camera.** Jackson renders a specific culture (Tuvalu), names a
> specific learning protocol ("10 to 20 new words each day"), then
> shows himself making a specific, embarrassing mistake (telling
> people the wrong thing about scales and walking sticks). This is
> Bourdain self-implication done at *missionary-anecdote density*.
> The detail is what makes it land. No abstract talk about humility;
> he just renders the moment.

> "One morning I called by mistake at the home of someone who was
> opposed to the truth. The householder, an older man who had been a
> teacher, was quick to remind me that we should not call at his home.
> Then, he said: 'I just want to mention one thing. In your translation,
> you use the passive form too much. It is not used that often in
> Tuvaluan.' I checked with others, and he was correct."
> 🏷 **Mr. Rogers possession-without-condition** at one remove —
> Jackson grants the opposer credit ("he was correct") without
> caveat, without softening, without re-establishing his authority
> after granting it. This is the move Mr. Rogers uses to grant the
> child credit; Jackson uses it to grant the *adversary* credit. Same
> structural move, harder application.

> "I can still hear the words of Saulo Teasi, an elderly Tuvaluan
> brother. After he had sung a Kingdom song for the first time in
> his language, he said: 'I think you should tell the Governing Body
> that these songs sound better in Tuvaluan than they do in English.'"
> 🏷 **Quoted-dialogue-as-landing** — Jackson lets a non-Western
> voice deliver his article's emotional peak. The closing words
> aren't his; they're Saulo's. Tyler doesn't currently use this move.

**Signature move (confirming brief's characterization)**

The brief says: *"Jackson: missionary-narrative with concrete cultural
detail."* Confirmed. The deeper Jackson signature is
**self-as-embarrassed-learner** — he repeatedly puts himself in
positions where he was wrong, was corrected (often by an outsider or
opposer), and the correction is the lesson. The Bourdain
self-implication mechanic, but at full extension: Jackson actually
lets the article's authority figure be Saulo, the opposer, the
mechanic-elder who quit his job to translate. He decentralizes his
own voice repeatedly. That's rare in GB-authored prose.

For Tyler: the actionable take is **let the other voice land the
beat**. When you've rendered a scene with a relative or a coworker,
let *their* line — not yours — be the line that carries the close.

### Mark Sanderson

**Sources accessed**

- Mark Sanderson. "Can We Speed Up the Day of Jehovah?" — Bethel
  morning worship, summarized/quoted in the July 2017 JW Broadcasting
  transcript (jwbroadcastingtranscripts-blog.tumblr.com). Verbatim
  spoken-prose passages preserved.
- Watchtower Online Library Sanderson index, wol.jw.org.
- Mark Sanderson. "Imitate Jesus and Remain at Peace" Watchtower
  Study, April 2019, jw.org (likely-authored study article; voice
  fingerprint matches; treat as supporting source).

**Mechanics inventory (3–5 tagged passages)**

> "Well, here's the question we want to ask this morning: Is it
> actually possible to speed up the day of Jehovah?"
> 🏷 **Tippett formative-origin question** delivered as
> *interrogation-of-the-text*. Sanderson's openers are almost always
> a single direct question framed as the day's working hypothesis.
> Restrained, low-temperature, careful.

> "Well, now, just as a quick reminder: What is godly devotion? Well,
> just looking at our New World Translation glossary, here's the
> definition: It's 'reverence, worship, and service to Jehovah God,
> with loyalty to his universal sovereignty.' Well, what do we get
> from that definition? Did you notice — reverence, worship, service?
> You see, godly devotion is not passive; it's active. It's reflected
> in action and in activity."
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Definition-then-redirect.** Sanderson reads a
> formal definition aloud, then asks "what do we get from that
> definition?" and redirects from the definition's words to one
> structural insight (*not passive; it's active*). Brown defines
> abstract terms ("uncertainty, risk, emotional exposure") to
> compress; Sanderson defines them to *redirect attention to a single
> verb-aspect* hidden inside. Different aim — Brown is naming, Sanderson
> is foregrounding.

> "Well, there are so many opportunities for us to imitate Jehovah
> and be hospitable — to share with others according to their needs.
> And this really is an expression not only for another person but
> it's an expression of our love for Jehovah — our desire to want to
> imitate him. Well, we all acknowledge that. But here's the question:
> When was the last time that you acted hospitably? Or when was the
> last time I acted hospitably?"
> 🏷 **Voss labeling + self-implication self-correction** — Sanderson
> asks "when was the last time *you*…" then immediately corrects to
> "Or when was the last time *I*…". The pivot from second-person
> diagnostic to first-person inclusion is deliberate and consistent
> across his talks. He never lets a question land on the audience
> without first landing it on himself. This is a Sanderson trademark.

> "You see, godly devotion is not passive; it's active."
> 🏷 **Sam Herd parallel-clause inversion** — straight reach into the
> default landing mechanic. Sanderson uses this restrainedly; one or
> two per talk, not every paragraph.

> "It's not enough just to love the quality of hospitality. If we
> are going to have deeds of godly devotion, we're going to have to
> practice it."
> 🏷 **Clear two-noun pivot** — *love-the-quality* vs.
> *practice-the-quality*. The same shape Splane uses, but Sanderson
> compresses it into one short two-sentence pair rather than building
> it across a paragraph.

**Signature move (filling in the brief's "?")**

The brief left Sanderson as a question mark. This pass: Sanderson's
signature is **second-person-then-first-person self-correction**
(*"When was the last time that you acted hospitably? Or when was the
last time I acted hospitably?"*). He's the only GB voice in this pass
who systematically refuses to let a diagnostic question land only on
the audience. He pulls it back to himself before letting it advance.

This is the **Voss-Bourdain self-implication** move, but operationalized
as a *grammatical reflex* rather than a deliberate one. Tyler should
borrow this as a slot-2 (label/parity) move when the comment is even
faintly diagnostic.

A second Sanderson signature, less common: **definition-then-redirect**
(read the formal definition; redirect attention to one aspect). Useful
when the article's paragraph already cites a definition or footnote
and Tyler can pivot off it.

### Kenneth Cook Jr.

**Sources accessed**

- "A New Member of the Governing Body" / Kenneth Cook, Jr. *The
  Watchtower (Study Edition)*, January 2019. jw.org. (Brief
  third-person announcement; no first-person voice.)
  https://www.jw.org/en/library/magazines/watchtower-study-january-2019/new-member-governing-body/
- Kenneth Cook, Jr. 2022 Convention Sunday public talk "Friendship
  With God — How Possible?" Verbatim quoted excerpts preserved at
  https://avoidjw.org/doctrine/2022-convention-sunday-review/
- Kenneth Cook, Jr. "Modesty — A Quality That Stems From 'What Is
  Good'" — referenced as available video; full transcript not
  accessed in this pass.

**Mechanics inventory (3–4 tagged passages — Cook source set is
thinner than the others)**

> "Jehovah promises that his friends will receive eternal life in
> peace. If that is what you yearn for, then please accept our
> invitation to study the Bible with Jehovah's Witnesses. By doing
> so, you will learn about an unending future when anguish of heart
> is gone forever, an unending future in which God will permanently
> wipe away tears from the faces of his friends. That future is not
> only possible, it is the future that God promises!"
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Pastoral-warm conditional invitation.** Cook
> consistently pairs *if [you yearn for X]* with *then [accept this
> invitation]* — not as sales-CTA but as a soft conditional that
> grants the listener their unspoken longing first. The "if that is
> what you yearn for" labels the emotion (Voss labeling) and offers
> a path *only if the labeled condition is true*. Mr. Rogers
> possession-without-condition at the structural level; Cook's tonal
> register is the warmest in the GB cohort.

> "Likewise, associating with those who also want to be God's friend
> is the right environment for us…."
> 🏷 **Bourdain preposition-pivot in pastoral key** — *with those
> who also want to be God's friend.* The qualifier *who also want to
> be* compresses an entire identity-and-direction picture into one
> adverbial phrase. Restraint at the sub-clause level.

> "An unending future when anguish of heart is gone forever, an
> unending future in which God will permanently wipe away tears from
> the faces of his friends."
> 🏷 **Brown triple-naming meets Mr. Rogers selective specification**
> — Cook repeats "an unending future" as an anaphora, then specifies
> *what kind* of future inside each repetition. He's not stacking
> three different images; he's clarifying *one* image by re-stating
> it with progressive selection.

**Signature move (filling in the brief's "?")**

The brief left Cook as a question mark. This pass: Cook's signature
is **pastoral-warm conditional invitation** — *"If that is what you
yearn for, then please…"* — and a tonal commitment to warmth that no
other GB member sustains for full talks. Where Lett is intense, Splane
analytical, Jackson narrative, Sanderson measured-careful, Morris
declarative-military, Cook is **warm-pastoral**. The grammatical
reflex underneath that warmth is the conditional invitation
(*if … then please …*).

This is the move Mr. Rogers uses constantly. Cook is the GB's closest
operational match to the Mr. Rogers register. Tyler can reach for Cook's
move when the paragraph is heavy with longing or doubt and the comment
needs to grant the longing without resolving it.

### Gerrit Lösch

**Sources accessed**

- Gerrit Lösch. "Losing a Father — Finding a Father." *The Watchtower
  (Study Edition)*, July 15, 2014. jw.org.
  https://www.jw.org/en/library/magazines/w20140715/earthly-father-heavenly-father/
- Gerrit Lösch, 2024 Governing Body Update commentary at avoidjw.org
  (cross-checked but not pulled for verbatim).

**Mechanics inventory (3–5 tagged passages)**

> "MY FATHER was born in Graz, Austria, in 1899, so he was a youth
> during World War I. He was conscripted into the German army soon
> after World War II broke out in 1939. He was killed in 1943 while
> fighting in Russia. Sadly, that is how I lost my father when I was
> only about two years old. I never had an opportunity to get to know
> him, and I very much missed having a father, especially when I
> realized that most other boys in school had one."
> 🏷 **NEW MECHANIC — Historical-frame compression.** Lösch opens
> with a one-paragraph compressed history (*1899 → 1914 → 1939 →
> 1943*) before naming the personal consequence. The dates do work:
> they ground the reader in scale before delivering the loss. Different
> from Splane's sequential progression (Splane is biographical
> step-by-step within his own life); Lösch is *world-historical
> framing first, then private grief*. Severe and Continental in tone.

> "Later, as a teenager, I gained comfort from learning about our
> heavenly Father, a superior Father who cannot die."
> 🏷 **Sam Herd parallel-clause inversion** with theological pivot —
> *earthly father lost / heavenly Father cannot die*. The whole
> article's argument lives in this one clause. Lösch uses it
> structurally rather than as a closing flourish.

> "His comment? 'Did Jesus and the apostles need a car to do
> full-time service?' That did it for me!"
> 🏷 **Quoted-question-as-landing** — Lösch lets the circuit
> overseer's rhetorical question (not his own) deliver the inflection
> point of his life. Same move as Jackson with Saulo Teasi; Lösch
> deploys it at the level of *life-shaping decision* rather than
> *cultural moment*. The willingness to credit another voice with the
> turning point is a recurring GB technique that the calibration.md
> hasn't named yet.

> "When brothers ask us how we met, we jokingly say, 'The branch
> office arranged for that.'"
> 🏷 **Dry humor as warmth signal** — single-line dry joke, no
> setup, no payoff explanation. Lösch's only moment of humor in the
> piece, and it does a lot of work — humanizes the German precision
> of the surrounding prose. Same instinct Sam Herd is described as
> having ("would say something serious then slide one in").

> "I look forward to observing from heaven the earthly resurrection
> and, at last, finding my physical father."
> 🏷 **Naval pursuit-order reframe** in eschatological key — the
> opening loss is *resolved* in the closing reunion, but the order
> of the sentence (*observing from heaven… at last, finding*) makes
> the reunion the rhetorical end-point of the whole life story. The
> arc compresses 80 years into one sentence.

**Signature move (filling in the brief's "?")**

The brief listed Lösch as "(if accessible)." This pass: Lösch's
signature is **historical-frame compression before private weight** —
he establishes the world-scale before naming the personal loss. The
compression of *1899 → 1939 → 1943 → "I lost my father when I was
about two years old"* is a Continental rhetorical move (severe, dated,
factual) that no other GB voice in this pass uses. American GB voices
(Lett, Cook, Sanderson) start at the personal moment; Splane and
Jackson zoom in from medium-narrative. Lösch zooms in from world-history.

This is a useful technique for Tyler when a paragraph is about
something with a long arc (kingdom history, exile, the 1914 cluster,
generational faithfulness). Don't open with the personal moment;
open with the dated frame, then collapse to the personal.

> "Yes, we — His spiritual family in heaven and all humans on earth
> who dedicate their lives to Him — give glory to Jehovah!"

signature. The audience-projection move is too platform-specific to

## Proposed additions to the 12-mechanic toolbox

This pass surfaced **6 new mechanics** that aren't covered in
`drafting-recipe.md`. Each is named, attributed to a primary GB
exemplar, and characterized for which slot it fills.

### 13. Cumulative-escalation hypothetical *(Lett)*
A single hypothetical scaled in stages until the listener's ability
to picture it breaks. Engine = exhaustion of imagination. **Slot:**
Landing (or Rotation if used as the talk's structural backbone).
**Reach for it when** the paragraph's concept is something genuinely
unimaginable (eternity, scale, a number of people, the depth of
forgiveness) and you want the listener to feel the limit of their
imagination as the rotation.

### 14. Reductio-by-vivid-image *(Lett)*
Refusal of an opposing position by re-stating it as a sensory
absurdity. The image is the refutation. **Slot:** Rotation.
**Reach for it when** the paragraph names a worldly position (chasing
the dollar, social-media validation, etc.) and you want to retire
the position rather than argue it. Higher risk than Morrison
frame-refusal — the image must be undignified enough to make the
position untenable but not so undignified it sounds preachy.

### 15. Permission-and-prohibition pair *(Splane)*
Two parallel sentences, each a complete rule, both derived from a
single upstream commitment. *"Where X, we [verb]. But where Y, we
must [opposite verb]."* **Slot:** Rotation or Landing.
**Reach for it when** the paragraph hinges on a balance the audience
keeps confusing in only one direction. Splane is the GB master of
this; it's the move Tyler doesn't have natively.

### 16. Definition-then-redirect *(Sanderson)*
Read a formal definition aloud, then redirect attention to one
structural insight inside it (often a verb-aspect — "*not passive;
it's active*"). **Slot:** Rotation.
**Reach for it when** the article paragraph already cites a definition
or footnote, or the study word is one the audience uses without
having looked at it (e.g., "discreet," "loyal love," "godly devotion").

### 17. Pastoral-warm conditional invitation *(Cook)*
*"If that is what you yearn for, then please [grant the listener
their unspoken longing first, then offer the path]."* **Slot:**
Landing (or Slot 2 in pastoral comments).
**Reach for it when** the paragraph carries longing or doubt and the
comment needs to grant the longing without resolving it. The closest
existing toolbox match is Mr. Rogers possession-without-condition;
Cook's version *names the condition explicitly* before granting the
permission.

### 18. Historical-frame compression *(Lösch)*
Open with a dated, world-historical paragraph before naming the
personal consequence. **Slot:** Opener.
**Reach for it when** the paragraph is about something with a long
arc (kingdom history, exile, generational faithfulness, persecution
across decades) and you want to ground the reader in scale before
delivering the personal weight. Severe register; use sparingly.

### Borderline / not new mechanics

- **Audience-projected dramatic monologue (Morris)** — too platform-specific
  for Tyler's audience-comment register. Don't add to toolbox.
- **Quoted-dialogue-as-landing (Jackson, Lösch)** — strong move, but
  it's a *deployment* of the existing scripture-paraphrase-lock
  technique aimed at non-scripture sources. Treat as a **variant** of
  scripture-paraphrase-lock rather than a new mechanic.
- **Polarization-against-ideas (Lett)** — already covered as Tate
  borrow-rule in calibration.md. Tyler shouldn't reach for this.

## Implications for Tyler's drafting

Five specific actionable instructions, mapped to the four-slot
architecture:

### 1. When the paragraph is about something genuinely unimaginable
**Reach for Lett's cumulative-escalation hypothetical (#13) in Slot
4.** Eternity, the number who'll be resurrected, the depth of
Jehovah's forgiveness, "before the foundation of the world." Pick a
small unit (a grain of sand, a coin, a step), iterate it through three
or four scaled stages, then close on *"and we've barely begun"*.
Restraint test: stop iterating before the math gets cute. The image
must overflow comprehension on the third or fourth stage, not the
sixth.

### 2. When the paragraph hinges on a confusion the audience keeps making in only one direction
**Reach for Splane's permission-and-prohibition pair (#15) in Slot
3.** *"Where the Bible makes a clear connection, we gladly accept
it. But where the Bible is silent, we must be silent."* Tyler
currently reframes ("the question isn't X — it's Y") which lets the
audience arrive at the answer; Splane *adjudicates*. When the comment
needs the audience to leave with a clear rule rather than a clear
question, switch to Splane.

### 3. When the comment is even faintly diagnostic
**Reach for Sanderson's first-person self-correction (#16-adjacent,
or Voss labeling already in toolbox) in Slot 2.** Never let *"when
was the last time you…"* land alone. Always pull it back to *"…or
when was the last time I…"*. This is a Sanderson grammatical reflex
that protects the comment from sounding judgmental. Tyler already
operates in *"I → we"* migration (calibration.md, Phrasebook stem
#6); this is the same instinct sharpened by one beat.

### 4. When the paragraph carries longing or doubt the audience can't yet name
**Reach for Cook's pastoral-warm conditional invitation (#17) in
Slot 4.** *"If that's what you've been longing for, the search itself
is part of the answer."* Tyler's current Slot-4 default is the Sam
Herd parallel-clause inversion. Cook's move is the upgrade when the
paragraph is heavy and the audience is carrying weight the article
isn't acknowledging. Tonal shift: slower, warmer, longer breath
between the conditional and the invitation.

### 5. When the paragraph references a key word the audience uses without having looked at it
**Reach for Sanderson's definition-then-redirect (#16) in Slot 3.**
Words like *discreet, loyal, hospitable, longsuffering, faith,
confidence* — the audience knows them but has stopped seeing them.
Quote the brief definition (footnote, glossary, NWT) and redirect to
one verb-aspect inside it. *"Notice it doesn't say felt; it says
acted."* This is the move Sanderson uses in his July 2017 talk
(*"godly devotion is not passive; it's active"*), and it lands
because the audience hears the definition fresh.

### Bonus — when the paragraph has a missionary or culturally-specific story
**Reach for Jackson's let-the-other-voice-land-the-beat move
(quoted-dialogue-as-landing, variant of scripture-paraphrase-lock).**
When you've rendered a scene with a relative or a coworker, let
*their* line — not yours — be the line that carries the close. Tyler
already does this implicitly with the brother and the spaghetti; do
it more deliberately. The comment lands harder when the closing voice
isn't yours.

### What NOT to borrow

- Morris's audience-projected dramatic monologue (too platform).
- Morris's theatrical exclamation landing.
- Lett's polarization-against-ideas in absolute mode (the Tate-style
  certainty without hedges; calibration.md has the existing rule).
- Lösch's historical-frame compression at full Continental severity
  (use a softened version only).
