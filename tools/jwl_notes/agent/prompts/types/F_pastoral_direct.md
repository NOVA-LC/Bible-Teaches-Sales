# Comment Type F — Pastoral Direct (no illustration, direct address)

> Read `_shared_core.md` first.

## When this type fits

The paragraph is touching a *real weight* the brother in the third row is carrying tonight — discouragement, doubt about whether Jehovah sees him, exhaustion, grief, regret. The listener doesn't need an illustration; he needs to be **directly named and addressed** with the verse-words doing the comforting in real time.

Good fits: Job's suffering, Hannah's barrenness, David's depression psalms, Elijah under the broom tree, the prodigal son's return, anything in Isaiah 40-55, paragraphs about Jehovah's care for the downcast.

**Not** for: practical method paragraphs (Type C), narrative-progression paragraphs (Type D), cultural-context paragraphs (Type H).

## The shape — three movements

```
[NAME THE WEIGHT]   →   [VERSE-WORDS AS DIRECT BALM]   →   [RENAMED STATE]
       ↓                      ↓                                ↓
"if you're carrying X      the verse acts on the              "tonight you walk out
tonight..."                 named weight in real time         carrying Y instead"
```

**No illustration. No third party. No domestic scene.** The brother IS the only character. The verse-words address him directly. The voice is the closest Tyler gets to Mr. Rogers — possession-without-condition.

## Voice signature

"If you've ever..." — Cook conditional invitation as the opener. Direct second-person all the way through. The verse is QUOTED, then **applied to the listener by name**: "When Isaiah says 'your light has come' — he's not talking about the nation, he's talking about you."

The lift is real. The brother walks in carrying weight; he walks out NAMING it differently — not "I'm a failure" but "I'm being seen." Not "my service is invisible" but "my service is faithful."

## Forbidden for Type F specifically

- ❌ Domestic illustration. Even briefly. Type F doesn't use third-party characters.
- ❌ Generic "we" framing. This is "you" — direct second-person. The brother is the only character besides the verse.
- ❌ Sentimentality without scripture. Pastoral encouragement that isn't anchored in verse-words is therapy, not Bible teaching.
- ❌ The lift before the weight is named. You can't release someone from a weight you didn't name first.

## Worked example — passing Type F

> *If you've spent the last three years showing up to every meeting, knocking on every door in your group's territory, and watching nothing happen — no study you can stick with, no return visits that go anywhere, no one walking up to you saying "your service helped me find Jehovah" — keep that weight with you for a second. Don't put it down yet. Now read Hebrews 6:10 with me. "God is not unrighteous so as to forget your work and the love you showed for his name." Not "won't forget." Cannot. Not "will reward someday." Already remembers. Not "if you do enough." Already counted. The verse isn't asking you to be more visible. It's telling you you already are. To him. Tonight when you go home and the weight comes back, name it back the way the verse names it: not invisible. Remembered. He's already counted what you did. Keep walking.*

**What works**: weight is named in detail (three years, no study, no return visits, no one saying it helped). No domestic illustration. Heb 6:10 is quoted then applied directly to the listener ("the verse isn't asking you to be more visible. It's telling you you already are."). Close-state renames the weight: "not invisible. Remembered."
