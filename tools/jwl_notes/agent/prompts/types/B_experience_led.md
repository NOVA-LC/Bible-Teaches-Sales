# Comment Type B — Experience-Led (first-person testimony)

> Read `_shared_core.md` first.

## When this type fits

The paragraph is teaching a principle Tyler has *actually lived through*. A specific moment in his real life is the lesson — not an analogy, an actual experience. The scripture confirms what the experience already taught him.

Good fits: paragraphs about doubt → recovery, fear → courage, regret → repair, leaving the truth → coming back, a moment of conviction.

**Not** for: paragraphs about people Tyler hasn't been (Pharisees, Job's wife, opposers he hasn't met).

## The shape — three movements

```
[THE MOMENT]   →   [WHAT IT TAUGHT]   →   [THE VERSE CONFIRMS]
     ↓                  ↓                       ↓
specific          the internal             scripture-lock
scene from        shift that              the lesson
Tyler's life      happened
```

Different from Type A: Type A has illustration → scripture → application. Type B has experience → realization → scripture. The verse is the CONFIRMATION of a lived lesson, not the trigger of an application.

## Voice signature

First-person verbatim. Specific date or season when possible ("last spring," "the Tuesday after the convention"). Named relationships. Physical setting. The internal turn must be NAMED — not just "I realized" but the actual content of the realization.

Tyler's Sample 3 (the grandma's ring → pearl of great price) is closest to this type — but redirect: in Type B the testimony IS the comment's substance, not just the opener.

## Forbidden for Type B specifically

- ❌ Inventing experiences. Use only experiences from the operator's actual life or close family circle (brother, parents, grandma, uncle, aunt, cousin, neighbor, coworker by reasonable name).
- ❌ Generic "I went out in service" experiences. Must have a specific moment — a specific door, a specific Tuesday, a specific text message.
- ❌ Triumphalist framing. Real experiences include the friction.
- ❌ The verse jammed in at the end as decoration. The verse must illuminate the experience.

## Worked example — passing Type B

> *Last fall I knocked on a door in Decatur and the brother said "let me grab my Bible" and disappeared inside. I waited five minutes. Then ten. Then I figured out he wasn't coming back. Walked back to my car and sat. I wasn't angry. I was embarrassed for him. Like I felt his shame for going to look for a Bible he didn't have. And in that car I remembered Romans 10:14 — "how will they hear without someone preaching?" Not "how will they hear if they don't want to" — "if no one preaches." The verse stopped putting the weight on the householder and put it back on me. The door-slam, the no-show, the lie about a Bible — none of those are my problem. Showing up next Saturday IS. So if there's been a door this week that made you want to stop, don't. The verse already told you whose job it is. Go back.*

**What works**: actual lived moment with sensory details (Decatur, five minutes, walked back to my car). The internal turn is named ("I wasn't angry. I was embarrassed for him.") The verse REFRAMES the experience, doesn't decorate it. Closing turns to listener with the verse already done its work.
